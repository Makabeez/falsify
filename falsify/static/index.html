Test a trading claim before you trade it.

Paste the post, drop a screenshot of the chart, or a short clip. Muse Spark turns it into exact rules, using only the claimant's words. Then nine years of real Binance prices try to break them.

Most claims don't survive. On Bitcoin's daily chart, a naive backtest calls most strategies that buy at random profitable, because Bitcoin went up. Falsify passes about one in twenty, which is what its threshold promises.

Or drop a screenshot or clip (PNG, JPG, WEBP or MP4), or click to choose a file.

Takes about a minute. Everything after the post date is data the claimant couldn't have seen.

    How a claim is tested

    The model reads; it never does the arithmetic. Every number on a result page comes from deterministic code you can run yourself with make judge-demo.

    1. Read. For screenshots and clips, Muse Spark first writes down every word and number it sees or hears.
    2. Compile. It turns the claim into rules in a small fixed language: indicators, thresholds, exits. No code.
    3. Check the quotes. Each rule must quote the claimant's exact words or say it was assumed. Paraphrases are sent back.
    4. Audit. A second Muse Spark pass compares the rules with the original and flags anything that changed. If something can't be expressed, the verdict says it tested an approximation.
    5. Escalate. The rules get two repair rounds for quotes and one for the audit. If the checks still disagree after that, the result is marked for a human check instead of being presented as settled. A trader's sign-off shows next to the verdict.
    6. Try to break it. Costs, 2,000 random-timing look-alikes, the period after the post, both halves of history, eleven other coins, and every reasonable reading of vague numbers.
    Not financial advice. A claim that survives has passed tests, not earned your money. Prices: Binance spot, public market data. Built for the Meta Global AI Developer Hackathon with Muse Spark.
